Start with the Commons Library hub, which collects many of the toolkits below. Then use the trackers to find what is proposed near you.

First week of a site fight

  • Find the zoning rules, the planning or council agenda, and the notice requirements for the proposed site. Check whether notice was given correctly.
  • Request planning, environmental, water and power documents through public records channels. Ask whether non-disclosure agreements are involved.
  • Ask for a public hearing, and prepare short testimony from several neighbors rather than one long speech.
  • Start a group that people can join in one step, and track proposals nearby with the trackers listed above.
  • Link up with groups in neighboring places. The toolkits from AlgorithmWatch, MediaJustice and the Data Center Working Group, all listed in the Commons Library hub, cover these steps.

Who is doing this work

People

Interviews and profiles of people working on AI, data and community rights. Each profile covers the person, their work and their community, in their own words.

How profiles are made

Their words first

Each profile centers an interview. People review the text and can correct it before it is published.

Consent and control

Each person decides what to share, including their name, photo, location and links. Some organizers face harassment, so a pseudonym or leaving details out is always an option.

Community, not only individuals

Each profile also describes the group, place or movement the person works with, and credits others who share the work.

Sources and dates

Claims about campaigns link to public reporting where it exists, and each profile shows when the interview took place.
